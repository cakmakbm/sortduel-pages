Sort Duel — Privacy Policy

Last updated 15 September 2026

Sort Duel does not ask for an account, an email address, or a real name. There is no advertising, no third-party analytics, and nothing that follows you across other apps or websites. This page describes everything the app stores or sends.

What stays on your device

  • Your best scores, today's result on the daily belt, how many days in a row you have played it, and the boards you have joined.
  • Your sound, haptics and button-side preferences.
  • The last three error messages, if the app has failed, so a problem can be described.
  • A random player identifier, kept in the device keychain so that it survives reinstalling. It is a random number and is not derived from you or your device.

What is sent, and when

Finishing any round of eight objects or more — including a practice round against the computer — sends a record of it to our server: the random player identifier, the nickname you chose, your score, the belt's seed and the times of your button presses. The seed and the presses are what let the server work the score out for itself rather than taking the app's word for it, and they are what somebody else races when a quick match finds nobody and hands them a saved run instead.

Playing online sends the same record, along with the progress readout your opponent watches during the round.

A nickname is optional and is shown to other players. Please do not put anything private in it.

Your rating

Quick matches move a rating kept against your identifier. Your nickname, that rating and your position on the ladder are shown to every other player on the rankings screen. Duels arranged between friends by invite code do not count towards it.

Counting players

Once a night our server counts how many people posted a score that day and how many of them had posted one before, and keeps those totals. It reads leaderboard rows that already exist, collects nothing extra, and the totals it keeps carry no identifier — they are counts, not a record of anybody. They exist to answer one question: whether enough people are playing for the game to be worth continuing.

Where it is kept, and for how long

On Amazon Web Services in Frankfurt, Germany. Daily leaderboard entries are deleted automatically after ten days and saved runs after thirty; a friend board lasts six months, and a rating and the nightly totals a little over a year. No data is sold, and none is shared with anyone except AWS, which hosts it on our behalf.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.

Removing your data

Deleting the app removes what is stored on your device, but not the random identifier: that is held in the device keychain, which iOS keeps, so reinstalling picks up the same one. To have everything held against it removed — leaderboard entries, saved runs and your rating — write to the address below with the identifier shown on the app's Settings screen.
